\begin{abstract}
Private inference of Transformer and Large Language Models (LLMs) via Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) faces a fundamental systems bottleneck: existing frameworks such as CrypTen execute secret-shared protocols over $\Zring$ via eager, operator-by-operator dispatch in PyTorch. While recent algorithmic advances---Wavelet-encoded Look-Up Tables (CuRL), 1-round Fourier-series activations and ODE Softmax (SHAFT), and hybrid plaintext evaluators (Fission)---substantially reduce communication rounds, their reference implementations remain fragmented across incompatible legacy PyTorch forks and suffer from severe kernel-launch and host--device synchronization overhead on modern accelerators.

We present \textbf{PriX}, an open-source JAX/XLA framework for private Transformer inference that unifies all four MPC approximation paradigms (\textbf{CrypTen}, \textbf{SHAFT}, \textbf{CuRL}, and \textbf{Fission}) under a single researcher-friendly API while compiling local cryptographic ring operations into fused XLA kernels. PriX introduces four systems optimizations: (1)~\emph{exact 4-limb FP64 matrix multiplication} over $\Zring$, leveraging accelerator floating-point tensor units without 53-bit mantissa overflow; (2)~\emph{whole-graph \texttt{@jax.jit} fusion} of multi-round MPC circuits, collapsing Kogge--Stone carry-lookahead rounds, multi-table wavelet LUT gathers, and Fourier/ODE recurrences into single compiled XLA kernels; (3)~\emph{batched single-round Beaver triple opening and wrap-count protocols}; and (4)~\emph{lock-free shared-memory and batched host-staged IPC} for multi-party collectives. Across 60 head-to-head benchmarks spanning 10 MPC primitives and 5 Transformer architectures (\texttt{BertTiny}, \texttt{BertBase}, \texttt{GPT2}, \texttt{Llama}, and \texttt{ModernBert}) on both CPU and NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU, PriX outperforms the PyTorch CrypTen baseline on \textbf{100\% of benchmarks (60/60)}, achieving \textbf{1.47$\times$--6.26$\times$} end-to-end LLM speedups on CPU, \textbf{1.97$\times$--3.09$\times$} LLM speedups on GPU, and up to \textbf{10.30$\times$} speedups on individual nonlinearities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

Deploying Transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs)~\cite{vaswani2017attention} on sensitive user prompts or proprietary model weights requires cryptographic privacy guarantees. Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) over the machine ring $\Zring$~\cite{mohassel2017secureml,rathee2020cryptflow2,knott2021crypten,hao2022iron} enables two or more distrusting parties to jointly evaluate neural networks on secret-shared inputs without revealing either the client's prompt or the server's parameters.

Over the past five years, CrypTen~\cite{knott2021crypten} has served as the foundational Python research platform for machine-learning-oriented MPC. Building upon CrypTen, a wave of recent cryptographic protocol innovations has dramatically improved the accuracy and round complexity of Transformer nonlinearities ($\operatorname{GeLU}$, $\operatorname{SiLU}$, $\operatorname{Softmax}$, $\operatorname{LayerNorm}$, $\operatorname{RMSNorm}$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CuRL}~\cite{santos2024curl} introduced Haar and Biorthogonal-2.2 Wavelet-encoded Look-Up Tables (LUTs) paired with probabilistic truncation ($\mathsf{TruncPr}$)~\cite{escudero2020improved}, compressing LUT communication and memory footprints while preserving high numerical fidelity.
    \item \textbf{SHAFT}~\cite{kei2025shaft} introduced 1-round trigonometric-triple Fourier-series evaluation for $\operatorname{GeLU}$, $\operatorname{SiLU}$, $\operatorname{Erf}$, $\operatorname{Sigmoid}$, and $\operatorname{Tanh}$, alongside a constant-round Forward-Euler Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) solver for $\operatorname{Softmax}$.
    \item \textbf{Fission}~\cite{ugurbil2025fission} introduced a hybrid permute-and-reveal architecture that offloads elementwise nonlinearities to a non-colluding plaintext evaluator party.
\end{itemize}

Despite these algorithmic breakthroughs, MPC research for Transformers faces two critical systems obstacles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ecosystem Fragmentation and Legacy Dependencies:} Original CrypTen has been archived since 2023 and is pinned to legacy PyTorch~1.9 and Python~3.7, while CuRL, SHAFT, and Fission reside in separate, mutually incompatible forks. Researchers conducting ablation studies across MPC approximation paradigms must port models between distinct codebases.
    \item \textbf{Eager Dispatch and Accelerator Underutilization:} In eager PyTorch, every elementary bitwise shift, mask, and ring addition in an MPC circuit dispatches an individual CPU or CUDA kernel. For example, a single 64-bit Kogge--Stone comparison~\cite{kogge1973parallel} executes 6 tree rounds of bitwise XOR and AND operations; in eager mode, this triggers over 18 tiny kernel launches interleaved with host-side Python control flow. Furthermore, because CUDA lacks native tensor-core support for 64-bit integer ($\texttt{int64}$) matrix multiplication, PyTorch's CrypTen forks decompose 64-bit matrices into four 16-bit limbs and execute \emph{16 separate FP64 \texttt{torch.matmul} kernels} per private matrix multiplication. Consequently, GPU execution in existing CrypTen forks is heavily bottlenecked by launch overhead and PCIe host--device synchronization.
\end{enumerate}

To address both challenges, we design and implement \textbf{PriX} (\emph{Private Accelerated Transformers in JAX}), a modern, drop-in alternative to CrypTen built on the JAX/XLA compiler stack~\cite{bradbury2018jax}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Multi-Paradigm MPC Framework:} PriX is the first open-source library to unify \textbf{CrypTen} polynomial/Newton--Raphson approximations~\cite{knott2021crypten}, \textbf{SHAFT} Fourier-series and ODE Softmax~\cite{kei2025shaft}, \textbf{CuRL} Wavelet LUTs and $\mathsf{TruncPr}$~\cite{santos2024curl,escudero2020improved}, and \textbf{Fission} hybrid evaluators~\cite{ugurbil2025fission} under a single API supporting Python~3.10--3.13 across CPUs, CUDA GPUs, and TPUs. Researchers can switch paradigms in one line of code (\texttt{mode="lut" | "shaft" | "crypten" | "fission"}) or compose hybrid configurations (e.g., SHAFT ODE Softmax with CuRL Wavelet LayerNorm).
    \item \textbf{Whole-Graph XLA Kernel Fusion for MPC Circuits:} PriX compiles local secret-share arithmetic, Kogge--Stone carry-lookahead rounds, vectorized multi-table wavelet LUT gathers, and Fourier/ODE recurrences via \texttt{@jax.jit}, eliminating intermediate memory traffic and Python dispatch overhead.
    \item \textbf{Exact 10-Pair FP64 $\Zring$ Matrix Multiplication \& Batched Collectives:} We reduce 64-bit ring matrix multiplication from 16 to 10 exact 16-bit limb products modulo $2^{64}$ inside a single fused XLA kernel, and coalesce Beaver triple openings $(\epsilon, \delta)$ and wrap-count reductions into single communication rounds backed by lock-free shared memory.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive 60-Benchmark CPU \& GPU Evaluation:} We evaluate PriX against the PyTorch CrypTen baseline across 10 primitives and 5 Transformer architectures (\texttt{BertTiny}, \texttt{BertBase}, \texttt{GPT2}, \texttt{Llama}, \texttt{ModernBert}) in all 4 approximation modes on both CPU and NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU. PriX is faster on all 60/60 benchmarks, achieving \textbf{1.47$\times$--6.26$\times$} LLM speedups on CPU and \textbf{1.97$\times$--3.09$\times$} on GPU.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and MPC Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Fixed-Point Encoding and Secret Sharing over $\Zring$}
Real-valued tensors $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k}$ are encoded into the ring $\Zring$ using a fixed-point scaling factor $S = 2^f$ (where $f = 16$ precision bits by default):
\begin{equation}
    \mathsf{Encode}(x) = \lfloor x \cdot 2^f \rceil \bmod 2^{64}, \qquad
    \mathsf{Decode}(\hat{x}) = \frac{\mathsf{Signed}_{64}(\hat{x})}{2^f}.
\end{equation}
Across $N$ parties (typically $N=2$), PriX maintains two secret-sharing representations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Additive Arithmetic Sharing ($\shareA{x}$):} Party $p \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ holds $\shareA{x}_p \in \Zring$ such that $\sum_{p=0}^{N-1} \shareA{x}_p \equiv \mathsf{Encode}(x) \pmod{2^{64}}$. Linear combinations $\shareA{x + y} = \shareA{x} + \shareA{y}$ and public multiplications $\shareA{c \cdot x} = c \cdot \shareA{x}$ require zero communication (followed by local or interactive fixed-point truncation).
    \item \textbf{XOR Binary Sharing ($\shareB{x}$):} Party $p$ holds $\shareB{x}_p \in \{0, 1\}^{64}$ such that $\bigoplus_{p=0}^{N-1} \shareB{x}_p = x$. Bitwise XOR ($\oplus$) and bit shifts ($\ll, \gg$) are local; bitwise AND ($\wedge$) requires 1 communication round via binary Beaver triples.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multiplication, Truncation, and Nonlinearity Paradigms}
\paragraph{Beaver Multiplication Triples.}
To compute the private product $\shareA{z} = \shareA{x \cdot y}$ (or matrix product $\shareA{X Y}$), the parties consume a pre-generated triple $(\shareA{a}, \shareA{b}, \shareA{c})$ with $c = a \cdot b \bmod 2^{64}$~\cite{beaver1991efficient}. Parties locally compute $\shareA{\epsilon} = \shareA{x} - \shareA{a}$ and $\shareA{\delta} = \shareA{y} - \shareA{b}$, open $(\epsilon, \delta)$ in a single communication round, and evaluate:
\begin{equation}
    \shareA{x \cdot y}_p = \shareA{c}_p + \epsilon \shareA{b}_p + \shareA{a}_p \delta + \mathbb{I}[p = 0] \cdot (\epsilon \delta) \pmod{2^{64}}.
    \label{eq:beaver}
\end{equation}
Because multiplying two $2^f$-scaled integers yields scale $2^{2f}$, the product must be truncated by $f$ bits:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{CrypTen Wrap-Count Truncation (\texttt{crypten}):} Parties locally arithmetic-shift their shares by $f$ bits and correct for unsigned ring wraps $\theta_x$ via a single-round masked wrap-count protocol~\cite{knott2021crypten}.
    \item \emph{Probabilistic Truncation (\texttt{egk}):} Following Escudero et al.~\cite{escudero2020improved} and CuRL~\cite{santos2024curl}, parties mask $\shareA{z}$ with a correlated random pair $(\shareA{r}, \shareA{r \gg f})$, reveal $z + r$, and subtract $\shareA{r \gg f}$ from $(z + r) \gg f$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Four Nonlinear Approximation Backends.}
Table~\ref{tab:modes} summarizes the four approximation modes unified in PriX:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CrypTen Mode (\texttt{mode="crypten"})}~\cite{knott2021crypten}: Approximates $\exp(x) \approx (1 + x / 2^m)^{2^m}$ via $m=8$ repeated squarings, $1/x$ and $1/\sqrt{x}$ via Newton--Raphson iterations, and $\operatorname{GeLU}(x)$ via Taylor-series $\operatorname{erf}(x/\sqrt{2})$.
    \item \textbf{SHAFT Mode (\texttt{mode="shaft"})}~\cite{kei2025shaft}: Evaluates $\operatorname{GeLU}$, $\operatorname{SiLU}$, $\operatorname{Erf}$, $\operatorname{Sigmoid}$, and $\operatorname{Tanh}$ via an odd/even Fourier series $\sum_{k=1}^{K} \beta_k \sin(k \omega x)$ in \emph{1 communication round} by revealing $u = x + r$ against a pre-shared trigonometric triple $(\shareA{r}, \shareA{\sin(k \omega r)}, \shareA{\cos(k \omega r)})$ and applying the angle-addition identity locally:
    \begin{equation}
        \shareA{\sin(k \omega x)} = \sin(k \omega u)\shareA{\cos(k \omega r)} - \cos(k \omega u)\shareA{\sin(k \omega r)}.
        \label{eq:shaft_trig}
    \end{equation}
    For $\operatorname{Softmax}$, SHAFT integrates the differential equation $y'(t) = y(t) \odot (x - \langle y(t), x \rangle)$ over $t \in [0, 1]$ using constant-round Forward-Euler steps without any division or reciprocal circuit.
    \item \textbf{CuRL Wavelet LUT Mode (\texttt{mode="lut"})}~\cite{santos2024curl}: Compresses lookup tables using Haar and Biorthogonal-2.2 wavelet transforms into coarse scaling/detail coefficient tables, evaluates secret-shared LUT lookups via one-hot rotating correlations, and performs piecewise-linear interpolation with $\mathsf{TruncPr}$.
    \item \textbf{Fission Evaluator Mode (\texttt{mode="fission"})}~\cite{ugurbil2025fission}: Masks and randomly permutes shares before revealing differences to a non-colluding plaintext evaluator party, which computes exact float32 nonlinearities and returns fresh secret shares.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Exact 10-Pair FP64 Matrix Multiplication over $\Zring$}
\label{sec:limb_matmul}
Modern GPUs provide high-throughput IEEE-754 floating-point matrix multiplication engines (\texttt{cuBLAS} / Tensor Cores), whereas native 64-bit integer (\texttt{int64}) matrix multiplication is unsupported by hardware tensor units and falls back to slow scalar loops on CPU or software emulation on GPU.

To execute exact $\Zring$ matrix multiplication $C = A B \bmod 2^{64}$ for $A \in \Zring^{M \times K}$ and $B \in \Zring^{K \times N}$ on accelerator floating-point units, we decompose each unsigned 64-bit integer into four 16-bit limbs:
\begin{equation}
    A = \sum_{i=0}^{3} A_i \, 2^{16i}, \quad B = \sum_{j=0}^{3} B_j \, 2^{16j}, \qquad A_i \in \{0, \dots, 2^{16}-1\}^{M \times K},\; B_j \in \{0, \dots, 2^{16}-1\}^{K \times N}.
\end{equation}
Crucially, because we only require the result modulo $2^{64}$, \textbf{any limb pair with $i + j \ge 4$ contributes a multiple of $2^{64}$ and vanishes identically in $\Zring$}:
\begin{equation}
    A B \equiv \sum_{i=0}^{3} \sum_{j=0}^{3 - i} (A_i B_j) \, 2^{16(i + j)} \pmod{2^{64}}.
    \label{eq:ten_limbs}
\end{equation}
Whereas CrypTen's CUDA implementation~\cite{knott2021crypten,santos2024curl} splits matrices into 4 limbs and dispatches all \textbf{16} pairwise \texttt{torch.matmul} calls via a Python double loop, PriX:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Computes only the \textbf{10 lower-triangular pairs} $(i, j)$ with $i + j \le 3$, immediately eliminating $37.5\%$ of the floating-point GEMM FLOPs.
    \item Casts the 4 limbs to IEEE-754 \texttt{float64} (which has a 53-bit exact integer mantissa) and fuses all 10 limb extractions, \texttt{jnp.matmul} contractions, bit-shifts, and modular accumulation into a single \texttt{@jax.jit} XLA kernel. Since each limb entry satisfies $A_i, B_j \le 2^{16}-1$, every inner product entry satisfies $(A_i B_j)_{m,n} < K \cdot 2^{32} \le 2^{53}$ for all contraction dimensions $K \le 2^{21}$ ($2{,}097{,}152$), guaranteeing \textbf{bit-exact $\Zring$ results} with zero rounding error.
    \item Fuses the Beaver triple combination $\shareA{C} + \epsilon \shareA{B} + \shareA{A}\delta + \mathbb{I}[p=0](\epsilon\delta)$ into a single compiled kernel.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Whole-Graph XLA Fusion of Kogge--Stone Carry-Lookahead Circuits}
\label{sec:koggestone}
Private comparisons ($\shareA{x} < 0$, $\operatorname{ReLU}$, $\operatorname{Max}$) require extracting the most significant bit (MSB) of a secret-shared ring element by converting $\shareA{x}$ into binary shares $\shareB{x}$ via a parallel prefix carry-lookahead adder~\cite{kogge1973parallel}. Over 64-bit words, the Kogge--Stone tree executes $\log_2(64) = 6$ rounds. In round $r \in \{0, \dots, 5\}$ with stride $s = 2^r$, parties update generate and propagate bit-vectors $(G, P)$ via:
\begin{equation}
    G^{(r+1)} = G^{(r)} \oplus \big(P^{(r)} \wedge (G^{(r)} \ll s)\big), \qquad
    P^{(r+1)} = P^{(r)} \wedge (P^{(r)} \ll s).
\end{equation}
In eager PyTorch, each round's shifts, masks, Beaver triple XORs, and AND combinations execute as separate GPU kernels, and the two AND gates on $G^{(r)}$ and $P^{(r)}$ are often evaluated sequentially. In PriX:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We pack both AND gates into a single stacked tensor of shape $(2, \dots)$ so each Kogge--Stone round performs \textbf{a single batched Beaver AND communication} across the two bit-vectors.
    \item We compile the entire round update---including bit-masking, shift alignment, Beaver triple XORs, and $G/P$ recombination---into two \texttt{@jax.jit} kernels (\texttt{\_ks\_prep\_gp} and \texttt{\_ks\_finish\_gp}), reducing host--device dispatches by over $3\times$ and speeding up GPU comparisons/ReLU by \textbf{3.31$\times$--3.74$\times$}.
    \item Similarly, Binary-to-Arithmetic ($\mathsf{B2A}$) conversion of the extracted sign bit uses a single-round bit-mask opening combined via a compiled \texttt{@jax.jit} kernel (\texttt{\_b2a\_combine}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fused Multi-Table Wavelet LUTs and SHAFT Recurrences}
\label{sec:lut_shaft_fusion}
In CuRL mode (\texttt{mode="lut"}), nonlinearities such as $\operatorname{InvSqrt}$, $\operatorname{Exp}$, $\operatorname{GeLU}$, and $\operatorname{SiLU}$ require clamping secret-shared inputs to valid domains, shifting fixed-point indices, gathering coarse scaling and detail wavelet coefficients from lookup tables, and interpolating. In PriX:
\begin{itemize}
    \item All wavelet lookup tables are pre-transferred to accelerator memory at initialization (\texttt{LookupTables(device)}).
    \item For multi-table operations such as tailored dual-grid $\operatorname{InvSqrt}$ and negative-domain $\operatorname{Exp}$, PriX fuses index shifting, wrapping modulo $2^b$, and simultaneous gathering across all 2 or 3 tables into a single \texttt{@jax.jit} kernel (\texttt{\_lut\_gather\_2}, \texttt{\_lut\_gather\_3}), followed by a single batched collective reveal.
    \item In SHAFT mode (\texttt{mode="shaft"}), the entire trigonometric-triple Fourier evaluation (Equation~\ref{eq:shaft_trig}), including broadcast multiplication of $\sin(k\omega u)$ and $\cos(k\omega u)$ against secret-shared triple tables, inner-product summation against Fourier coefficients $\beta_k$, and polynomial tail combination, is compiled into a single XLA kernel (\texttt{\_fourier\_eval\_step}), yielding a \textbf{3.64$\times$} GPU speedup on $\operatorname{GeLU}$ and \textbf{3.30$\times$} on $\operatorname{SiLU}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Batched Single-Round Collectives and Lock-Free Shared Memory}
\label{sec:collectives}
Every private multiplication and matrix multiplication opens two masked tensors: $\epsilon = x - a$ and $\delta = y - b$. Rather than issuing two sequential \texttt{all\_reduce} calls (which doubles synchronization latency), PriX batches $[\epsilon, \delta]$ into a single collective call (\texttt{batched=True}). When $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ have different shapes (as in matrix multiplication where $\epsilon \in \Zring^{M \times K}$ and $\delta \in \Zring^{K \times N}$), PriX flattens and concatenates both arrays on-device via a compiled \texttt{@jax.jit} kernel before staging to host memory in a \textbf{single PCIe DMA transfer}, exchanges the single contiguous buffer across parties, and splits the result back on-device.

For 2-party local execution, \texttt{InProcessCommunicator} implements a \textbf{4-slot lock-free shared-memory ring buffer} backed by OS shared memory (\texttt{multiprocessing.RawArray}) and a lightweight 1-byte pipe token handshake. Each party writes its int64 buffer into slot $s \equiv \text{step} \pmod 4$, signals readiness via a 1-byte pipe write, and sums its peer's slot directly from shared memory without acquiring Python \texttt{multiprocessing.Barrier} locks or pickling tensors through OS queues.

\section{Researcher Ergonomics and Unified API}
\label{sec:api}
%======================================================================

A central design goal of PriX is enabling cryptography and machine learning researchers to prototype and benchmark private Transformer protocols with minimal boilerplate. Listing~\ref{lst:quickstart} illustrates how a complete multi-party ablation study comparing all four MPC approximation paradigms on a pre-norm Transformer block is written in PriX.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=pythonstyle, caption={Evaluating a private Transformer block across all four MPC approximation paradigms in PriX.}, label={lst:quickstart}]
import prix
import prix.nn as nn

class TransformerBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model=64, n_heads=4, d_ff=256):
        super().__init__()
        self.ln1 = nn.LayerNorm(d_model)
        self.attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(d_model, n_heads)
        self.ln2 = nn.LayerNorm(d_model)
        self.ffn = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(d_model, d_ff), nn.GELU(), nn.Linear(d_ff, d_model))

    def forward(self, x):
        h = self.ln1(x)
        x = x + self.attn(h, h, h)
        return x + self.ffn(self.ln2(x))

def evaluate_private_block():
    # Party 0 encrypts model weights; Party 1 secret-shares token embeddings
    model = TransformerBlock().encrypt(src=0)
    x = prix.array(prix.randn(1, 16, 64).reconstruct(), src=1)
    return model(x).reconstruct()

for mode in ["crypten", "shaft", "lut", "fission"]:
    out = prix.run(evaluate_private_block, world_size=2, mode=mode)
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Security Considerations and Limitations}
\label{sec:security}
%======================================================================

PriX is designed as a research framework operating under the standard \textbf{semi-honest (honest-but-curious)} security model:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Correlated Randomness Providers (\texttt{TFP} vs.\ \texttt{TTP}):} Like CrypTen~\cite{knott2021crypten}, PriX defaults to the Trusted First Party (\texttt{TFP}) provider for benchmarking convenience, where Party~0 locally generates Beaver triples and wavelet LUT shares. Because Party~0 knows the dealer randomness in \texttt{TFP} mode, \texttt{TFP} provides zero confidentiality against a curious Party~0 and must only be used for performance measurement. Setting \texttt{cfg.mpc.provider = "TTP"} spawns an independent dealer process (\texttt{TTPServer}) under the standard non-colluding third-party assumption.
    \item \textbf{Pseudorandom Number Generation:} To maximize throughput during benchmarking, PriX currently generates correlated share streams using vectorized \texttt{SplitMix64} on GPU and \texttt{PCG64} on CPU seeded from OS entropy (\texttt{secrets.randbits(64)}) exchanged during initialization. While fast, \texttt{SplitMix64} and \texttt{PCG64} are not cryptographically secure pseudorandom functions (CSPRFs); replacing them with XLA's counter-based \texttt{Threefry2x32} or hardware \texttt{AES-CTR} is planned for deployments requiring cryptographic indistinguishability.
    \item \textbf{Probabilistic Truncation Bounds:} In \texttt{lut} mode, $\mathsf{TruncPr}$~\cite{escudero2020improved} uses 64-bit ring masks. With $f=16$ fractional bits, inputs must remain bounded away from $2^{47}$ to avoid unsigned MSB wrap errors during probabilistic truncation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

We presented \textbf{PriX}, a modern JAX/XLA framework for privacy-preserving Transformer and LLM research via Secure Multi-Party Computation. By unifying four major MPC approximation paradigms (\textbf{CrypTen}, \textbf{SHAFT}, \textbf{CuRL}, and \textbf{Fission}) with whole-graph XLA kernel fusion, 10-pair exact FP64 ring matrix multiplication, and lock-free shared-memory collectives, PriX outperforms the PyTorch CrypTen baseline across all 60 CPU and GPU benchmarks by \textbf{1.47$\times$--6.26$\times$ on CPU} and \textbf{1.97$\times$--10.30$\times$ on GPU}. PriX is open-source and available on PyPI via \texttt{pip install prix-jax}.
